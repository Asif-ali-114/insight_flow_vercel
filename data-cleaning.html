Ten problems we look for

Cleaning improves consistency and usability before analysis. It does not make a poor dataset perfect, and it never fills gaps with invented numbers.

Missing values

Blank products, prices or dates. Treated as unknown, never silently set to zero.

Duplicate rows

The same transaction exported twice. Removed only when every key field matches exactly.

Inconsistent capitalization

“laptop”, “Laptop” and “LAPTOP” split one product into three in a pivot table.

Extra spaces

Leading or trailing spaces that make identical names look different.

Different date formats

2026-01-05, 05/01/2026 and 2026/01/05 in one column. We state the convention (day-first here) instead of guessing.

Incorrect data types

Numbers stored as text, such as “45 USD” or “1,180”.

Invalid categories

Values outside an agreed list, such as a region that does not exist.

Impossible or suspicious values

Dates like 30 February, quantities of 2.5 units, or negative quantities that may be returns.

Inconsistent product names

Several spellings of one product, mapped to an agreed list.

Incorrect numerical formatting

Currency symbols, thousands separators and mixed decimal styles.

Our cleaning rules, in plain language

What we change automatically

  • Capitalization and extra spaces in names.
  • Date style, converted to yyyy-mm-dd under the stated convention.
  • Currency text such as “$15.00” or “45 USD”, converted to a plain number.

What we never change on our own

  • Missing values: no zero, average or guess is filled in.
  • Negative quantities: they may be returns, so we ask.
  • Impossible dates and fractional quantities: kept and flagged.
  • Possible duplicates that are not exact matches.
